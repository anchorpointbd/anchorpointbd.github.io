A small demonstration

The first question is not always the real one.

This is a constructed example of the reasoning—not a client project or a design recommendation.

What you might ask
“The supplier says the larger pump is safer. Should we accept it?”

Before choosing a pump, we would make the operating condition visible:

  • What duty point must the system actually meet?
  • How will flow and resistance vary?
  • What do efficiency, wear, power and lifecycle cost change?
  • What failure are we trying to prevent?

The clearer questionWhat operating condition are we designing for—and which option meets it with acceptable consequence?

Our thinking

A clear route through a difficult decision.

The sequence stays consistent; the depth depends on the question.

  1. 01Understand

    What must become possible—and for whom?

  2. 02Diagnose

    What is a symptom, and what is creating it?

  3. 03Explore

    Which realistic routes and trade-offs exist?

  4. 04Decide

    What does the evidence support, and who owns the choice?

  5. 05Deliver

    What usable action and record should remain?
