Before increasing capacity, we separate three connected questions:

  • What performance is actually required?
  • Under which operating condition must it be achieved?
  • Which existing constraint is limiting the result?

Our thinking

A clear route through a difficult decision.

The sequence stays consistent; the depth depends on the question.

A solution chosen too early can make the real problem more expensive.
01

Understand

What must become possible, for whom, and what is already known?

Example: Agree what success looks like before discussing solutions.
02

Diagnose

What is the real cause, and what is only a symptom?

Example: Separate a recurring equipment symptom from the system condition creating it.
03

Explore

Which realistic options exist, and what trade-offs come with each?

Example: Compare repair, replacement, operating change, or a different technical route.
04

Decide

Which option does the evidence actually support?

Example: Make assumptions, consequences, cost, risk and constraints visible before choosing.
05

Deliver

What useful action, record or next step should remain?

Example: Leave a clear next move, decision note, review comment or action list.
