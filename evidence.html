Example decisions

Three questions worth slowing down for.

Illustrative scenarios—shown to explain our method, not presented as past client work.

VESSEL DECISIONRepair, replace—or redefine the mission?
THE REAL QUESTION

Which route restores dependable service for the required operating profile at an acceptable lifecycle risk—not merely the lowest initial cost?

What we would examine

  • Operating profile and service failures
  • Hull, machinery and statutory condition
  • Downtime and remaining-life assumptions
  • Shipyard capacity and material lead times
  • CAPEX, OPEX and disposal consequences

Options to compare

  • Targeted rehabilitation
  • Life-extension refit
  • Replacement vessel
  • Mixed fleet strategy
  • Mission or schedule redesign

What you would receive

  • Assumption and evidence register
  • Option matrix with sensitivity
  • Interface and decision map
  • Independent recommendation
  • Named escape routes
FACTORY DECISIONThe cheapest equipment is delaying production.
THE REAL QUESTION

Which procurement route protects production availability after installation, integration, spares, energy, maintenance and failure recovery are counted?

What we would examine

  • Duty point and operating envelope
  • Interface drawings and utilities
  • Failure and maintenance history
  • Vendor deviations and exclusions
  • Acceptance and warranty terms

Options to compare

  • Lowest compliant bid
  • Higher-efficiency alternative
  • Standardized installed base
  • Service-backed supply
  • Repair or retrofit existing asset

What you would receive

  • Normalized technical comparison
  • Lifecycle cost ranges
  • Commissioning evidence plan
  • Commercial risk questions
  • Approval-ready decision memo
PUBLIC INVESTMENT DECISIONA budget exists. The requirement does not.
THE REAL QUESTION

What public outcome must the investment reliably produce, for whom, under which constraints—and what must be known before procurement begins?

What we would examine

  • Mandate and intended beneficiaries
  • Demand, site and service data
  • Stakeholder and approval map
  • Budget basis and time constraints
  • Operations and maintenance owner

Options to compare

  • New asset
  • Upgrade existing capacity
  • Phased programme
  • Service or performance contract
  • Non-asset intervention

What you would receive

  • Outcome-based requirement brief
  • Options and consequences
  • Readiness and data-gap register
  • Decision-gate roadmap
  • Traceable budget assumptions

Capability boundaries

We coordinate authority.
We do not impersonate it.

Anchor Point can

  • Frame complex decisions and evidence needs
  • Interrogate requirements, reports and proposals
  • Compare options and lifecycle consequences
  • Coordinate disciplines and decision records
  • Prepare decision-ready briefs and questions

Anchor Point will not

  • Invent credentials, projects, partners or approvals
  • Replace appointed designers, class, regulators or certifiers
  • Guarantee outcomes outside controlled scope
  • Use public AI for confidential project decisions
  • Present a demonstration as completed client work

Before appointment

  • Scope, exclusions and decision authority are written
  • Named competency is matched to the assignment
  • Conflicts and data classification are agreed
  • Required standards and reviewers are identified
  • Deliverables and acceptance criteria are defined
