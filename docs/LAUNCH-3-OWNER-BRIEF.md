## Engineering Hub becomes the ecosystem entry

Passive strategic goal: reach engineers through recurring utility until Anchor Point becomes the natural first interface for daily engineering questions.

Anchor Point owns:

- the sorting, search and discovery experience;
- the taxonomy and quality labels;
- original calculators, formulas, process explanations and short notes;
- favourites/recent activity stored locally where practical;
- the conversation and optional relationship layer.

Anchor Point does not copy or re-host third-party standards, papers, university theses, library collections or proprietary calculators. It links to the authoritative publisher and stores only necessary catalogue metadata such as title, URL, publisher, discipline, access type, provenance and last-verification date.

## Ecosystem information architecture

Use an academically familiar progression without representing Anchor Point as a degree-awarding university:

1. **Faculty / domain** — broad world of work.
2. **Department / discipline** — professional field.
3. **Pathway** — Learn, Calculate, Convert, Find Sources, Apply at Work, Compare Decisions.
4. **Tool or source** — the actual calculator, converter, explainer, library catalogue, repository or official dataset.

Engineering is the deep first faculty. Initial departments should cover civil/infrastructure, mechanical/manufacturing, naval/marine, electrical/electronics, energy/environment, computing/systems, and materials/welding. Business/projects, finance/economics, law/governance and human systems/arts receive limited foundation entries now and expand later.

The future tool architecture has four distinct desks:

1. Scientific Calculator.
2. Unit and Conversion Tools — one verified engine exposed through 200+ useful sector-oriented routes, not 200 copied pages.
3. Engineering Calculators — formula, substitution, process, assumptions, limitations and verification prompts.
4. Knowledge and Sources — authoritative external resources linked at source.

## Mobile app direction

- Build web-first as an installable, offline-capable PWA.
- Package later for Android and iOS from the shared web codebase.
- Core tools remain usable without registration.
- Do not use manipulative notifications or a compulsory data gate. Earn habit through speed, offline usefulness, favourites, recent tools and a universal command bar.
- Optional profiles and follow-up must use explicit consent, data minimisation, deletion controls and truthful purpose statements.
- Usage may indicate interest; it must not be represented as proof of professional competence.

## Idea Graveyard

Purpose: let people unload undeveloped ideas without requiring them to perform polish or certainty. Anchor Point may periodically select serious ideas for a structured orientation response.

Required boundaries before live collection:

- submitter chooses private, anonymous-to-public, or attributable treatment;
- no promise that every idea will be reviewed, developed, funded, protected or answered;
- no confidential employer/client material, classified information, personal data about others or trade secrets;
- ownership/licence, retention, deletion, moderation and contact permission must be explicit;
- selected ideas require human review before publication or response;
- the MVP may demonstrate the process without accepting live submissions until policy and moderation operations are approved.

## Private release / frustration concept

This is an option under evaluation, not yet an institutional command or mental-health service.

Safest initial concept: a local-only **Release Pad** where a person types what they need to unload and deliberately erases it. The text is not transmitted or stored. It must not claim therapy, counselling, diagnosis, confidentiality beyond the technical truth, or emergency support.

A future share-with-a-human route would require separate explicit consent, trained moderation, safeguarding, escalation rules, retention limits and a clear response-time promise. Do not silently mix emotional disclosures into marketing or lead scoring.

## Owner review standard

Do not protect previous design decisions from criticism. Six hundred questions are expected to become institutional memory, evidence or reversible decisions. The website is not launch-ready merely because it is visually complete.
