How They Work

The reasoning should
remain visible.

Decisions are easier to trust when another person can see what was known, what was assumed, which alternatives were considered and why the next move was chosen.

A clear answer matters. An inspectable path to it matters too.

Our Working Promise

Reasoning you can inspect.

We do not promise every engineering problem will have a simple answer. We do promise the reasoning will be clear enough to inspect, challenge and explain to someone else.

  • We separate facts from assumptions.
  • We show more than one credible path when one exists.
  • We explain uncertainty instead of hiding it.
  • We keep the reason behind the recommendation.

Using the Method

What each stage must protect.

Home introduces the five-stage route. Here the focus is the discipline required inside each stage.

  1. UNDERSTAND

    Protect the question boundary.

    Define the decision, intended outcome, people affected and what is outside scope.

  2. DIAGNOSE

    Protect the evidence.

    Separate observed facts, supplied statements, assumptions, symptoms and possible causes.

  3. EXPLORE

    Protect credible alternatives.

    Keep more than one path visible where evidence supports it, including the option to check further.

  4. DECIDE

    Protect the reason.

    Connect the recommendation to criteria, consequences, uncertainty and authority.

  5. DELIVER

    Protect continuity.

    Record the next action, owner, verification need and trigger for review.

How We Check Ourselves

Test the reasoning before recommending.

Before recommending a direction, we test the reasoning for gaps, assumptions, missing evidence, and authority.

01

What do we know?

Separate verified evidence from statements, impressions and unconfirmed data.

02

What are we assuming?

Show every assumption that materially influences the result.

03

What could change the answer?

Test sensitivity to uncertainty, operating conditions and new facts.

04

What evidence is missing?

State the confidence limit and whether more checking is justified.

05

Who has authority to decide?

Separate technical advice, specialist verification, client approval and statutory responsibility.

COMPACT ILLUSTRATION

Initial recommendation: replace the equipment.

After the checks: operating data is incomplete, the assumed duty is unverified and specialist confirmation is required. The stronger recommendation becomes: instrument the system, confirm duty and review the result before replacement.

Evidence Discipline

Do not let different kinds of information blur together.

Confidence improves when every important input has a visible status and owner.

VERIFIED

Evidence we can trace.

Measured data, controlled documents, confirmed records and direct observations.

SUPPLIED

Statements awaiting confirmation.

Information received from people or documents whose status is not yet verified.

ASSUMED

Inputs used temporarily.

Assumptions kept visible because they may change the recommendation.

MISSING

Evidence that limits confidence.

Unknowns that justify checking, conditions or a narrower conclusion.

Who Decides What

Keep advice, verification and approval visible.

Anchor Point advises and compares. A specialist verifies where licensed authority is required. The client approves within the organization, and statutory authority stays with the regulated party.
